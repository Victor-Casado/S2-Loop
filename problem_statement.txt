S1. Get a simulated vehicle driving itself to a set of randomized goal points in Gazebo, without you touching the controls, while it avoids obstacles in its path.

Detailed Description:
Build a simulation, running on ROS 2 Jazzy with Gazebo, in which:

A vehicle starts at some origin point. A simple moving cube/box as the vehicle is completely fine, this is not a modeling exercise
A set of randomized XY coordinates is generated as waypoints for it to visit, in sequence.
The environment between waypoints contains obstacles ex. walls, hills/ramps that the vehicle must not drive through.
The vehicle navigates to each waypoint autonomously: no keyboard teleop, no manual driving. This should be a node (or set of nodes) that takes in the current position, the next goal, and whatever obstacle/sensor info you choose to use, and publishes movement commands on its own.

You have design freedom here: how you detect obstacles (a real sensor plugin, a simplified costmap, raycasting, or just hardcoded knowledge of wall positions) and how you plan paths (something as simple as move-toward-goal-and-turn-if-blocked is acceptable — you are not expected to reimplement Nav2).

Deliverable:
Install the Following:

Ubuntu 24.04 LTS
ROS 2 Jazzy Jalisco
Gazebo (the version that pairs with Jazzy — Gazebo Harmonic)
colcon + standard ROS 2 dev tools

(How you get these running  ie. VM, dual boot, WSL2, etc. is up to you. However, we strongly recommend dual booting or a VM as WSL is known to have issues.)

Document in your log:

A screen recording link of the simulation running in Gazebo, showing the vehicle visiting at least 3 waypoints in one run, going around at least one obstacle.
The launch file(s) and any world/model files needed to reproduce your sim. These should be pushed to a public GitHub repository and the link should be in your log.
A short explanation of how your navigation logic decides where to go and how it detects/avoids obstacles.
How did you generate the random waypoints, and how does the vehicle know it has "arrived" at one (what's your tolerance/threshold)?
How does your obstacle avoidance actually work — what does the vehicle "see," and what does it do when something is in the way?
What happens if a waypoint is unreachable or the vehicle gets stuck? Did you handle that, or did you discover it by watching it happen?
What would you change if this had to run on real hardware instead of in Gazebo?
